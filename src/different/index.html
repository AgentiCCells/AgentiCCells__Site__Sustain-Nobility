What makes it different

Six disciplines, one readiness check

AI governance is rarely only a legal question, or only a technical one. SUSTAIN-NOBILITY's site says its approach combines six areas of expertise, so an SME gets one joined-up view instead of six separate opinions.

The six areas

The area names are SUSTAIN-NOBILITY's. The one-line explanations under each are AgentiCCells's plain-language summaries of what each field usually covers, not their words.

1

AI governance

Who decides how AI is used, who is accountable, and how decisions are recorded.

2

Regulatory and legal risk

Which laws and rules apply to the way an organisation builds, buys or uses AI.

3

AI assurance and audit readiness

Being able to show evidence that controls exist and work, when someone asks.

4

Human rights and ethical safeguards

Protecting the people affected by AI decisions: fairness, privacy, dignity and redress.

5

Public policy and emerging regulation

Knowing what is changing, so today's choices still hold next year. See the context page.

6

SME operational realities

Advice sized for small teams with limited time and budget, not for large compliance departments.
