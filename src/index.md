## Six areas, one Readiness Check

“Most AI governance solutions focus on only one of these dimensions: Legal compliance, technical controls, or policy.”

1. **AI governance**: Who decides how AI is used, who is accountable, and how decisions are recorded. (AgentiCCells's plain-language summary)
2. **Regulatory and legal risk**: Which laws and rules apply to the way an organisation builds, buys or uses AI. (AgentiCCells's plain-language summary)
3. **AI assurance and audit readiness**: Being able to show evidence that controls exist and work, when someone asks. (AgentiCCells's plain-language summary)
4. **Human rights and ethical safeguards**: Protecting the people affected by AI decisions: fairness, privacy, dignity and redress. (AgentiCCells's plain-language summary)
5. **Public policy and emerging regulation**: Knowing what is changing, so today's choices still hold next year. (AgentiCCells's plain-language summary)
6. **SME operational realities**: Advice sized for small teams with limited time and budget, not for large compliance departments. (AgentiCCells's plain-language summary)

## The rules and standards around the question

- AgentiCCells's summary: The EU AI Act applies in stages, and an amending regulation, the Digital Omnibus on AI, has moved some of them. It gives SMEs simpler documentation and priority access to regulatory sandboxes, and caps their fines at the lower of two amounts.
- AgentiCCells's summary: The UK has no single AI law. Existing regulators apply five cross-sector principles; the government asked regulators to publish plans for supporting safe AI innovation, and chose to write governance guidance for SMEs instead of publishing its AI Management Essentials tool. The ICO's guidance says most uses of AI will need a data protection impact assessment.
- AgentiCCells's summary: Two voluntary frameworks are widely used to organise AI governance evidence: ISO/IEC 42001, a certifiable AI management system standard, and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, built around Govern, Map, Measure and Manage.
- AgentiCCells's summary: Taken together, the sources suggest what an SME may be asked to show: a record of the AI systems it uses and their risk class, AI literacy measures, impact assessments where personal data is involved, transparency notices and, for high-risk uses, human oversight.
- AgentiCCells's summary: Their Our Tool page says the check is aligned with the main UK and EU regulators relevant to AI, without naming them.

The dates, each with its source and read date, are on https://sustain-nobility.agenticcells.site/context/ and https://sustain-nobility.agenticcells.site/research/. This is not legal advice.
