European Union

EU AI Act and the Digital Omnibus on AI

AgentiCCells's summaryThe EU AI Act applies in stages, and an amending regulation, the Digital Omnibus on AI, has moved some of them. It gives SMEs simpler documentation and priority access to regulatory sandboxes, and caps their fines at the lower of two amounts.

Facts on the research page: f1, f2, f4, f5, f6, f7, f11, f12, f13, f15. Sources: 1 2 3

United Kingdom

Regulators, guidance and the ICO

AgentiCCells's summaryThe UK has no single AI law. Existing regulators apply five cross-sector principles; the government asked regulators to publish plans for supporting safe AI innovation, and chose to write governance guidance for SMEs instead of publishing its AI Management Essentials tool. The ICO's guidance says most uses of AI will need a data protection impact assessment.

Facts on the research page: f20, f21, f24, f25, f26, f27, f28. Sources: 4 5 6 7 8 9 10

Standards and frameworks

ISO/IEC 42001 and the NIST AI RMF

AgentiCCells's summaryTwo voluntary frameworks are widely used to organise AI governance evidence: ISO/IEC 42001, a certifiable AI management system standard, and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, built around Govern, Map, Measure and Manage.

Facts on the research page: f19, f30, f31. Sources: 11 12

What an SME may be asked to show

AgentiCCells's summaryTaken together, the sources suggest what an SME may be asked to show: a record of the AI systems it uses and their risk class, AI literacy measures, impact assessments where personal data is involved, transparency notices and, for high-risk uses, human oversight.

Facts on the research page: f32. Sources: 2
